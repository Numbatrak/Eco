—  SECTION  04  —
 
Wallet  —  What  It  Should  Look  Like   
Wallet  is  the  money-in  view.  It  shows  the  founder  exactly  what  revenue  has  been  
generated,
 
what
 
has
 
been
 
collected,
 
what
 
is
 
pending,
 
and
 
what
 
has
 
been
 
released.
 
It
 
is
 
not
 
a
 
payment
 
processor
 
—
 
it
 
is
 
a
 
tracking
 
and
 
visibility
 
layer
 
for
 
all
 
money
 
flowing
 
into
 
the
 
business
 
from
 
delivered
 
orders.  
What  Wallet  Shows  •  Total  revenue  generated  —  sum  of  all  delivered  order  values  in  the  selected  
period •  Total  collected  —  payments  confirmed  received  from  COD  remittances  and  
online
 
payments •  Pending  remittance  —  orders  delivered  but  cash  not  yet  confirmed  received  from  
agent •  Released  to  business  —  confirmed  received  and  reconciled •  Breakdown  by  payment  method  —  COD  vs  online  payment •  Breakdown  by  time  period  —  today,  this  week,  this  month,  custom  range •  Per-order  payment  status  —  which  specific  orders  are  paid,  pending,  or  
outstanding  
Remittance  Flow  for  COD  When  a  COD  order  is  delivered,  cash  is  collected  by  the  delivery  agent.  The  agent  
remits
 
to
 
the
 
business.
 
The
 
Wallet
 
view
 
tracks
 
this
 
as:  1.  Order  marked  as  delivered  in  the  Waybills  section 2.  Order  appears  in  Wallet  as  Pending  Remittance 3.  Business  owner  or  admin  confirms  cash  received  from  agent 4.  Order  moves  to  Collected 5.  Revenue  is  now  reflected  in  the  profitability  dashboard  This  flow  ensures  the  dashboard  never  shows  revenue  that  has  not  actually  been  
received
 
—
 
which
 
is
 
a
 
critical
 
distinction
 
for
 
COD
 
businesses
 
where
 
orders
 
can
 
be
 
returned
 
after
 
being
 
marked
 
delivered.

—  SECTION  05  —
 
Dashboard  —  What  the  Metrics  Mean   
The  dashboard  is  the  most  important  screen  in  Numbatrak.  Everything  here  is  
auto-calculated
 
from
 
data
 
already
 
entered
 
in
 
other
 
sections.
 
The
 
business
 
owner
 
should
 
not
 
need
 
to
 
calculate
 
anything
 
manually.  Filters:  by  product  (dropdown  —  All  Products  or  specific  product)  and  by  date  range  
(Today,
 
This
 
Week,
 
This
 
Month,
 
Custom).  
The  10  Core  Metrics  —  Definitions  and  Formulas   
Total  Orders  Generated
 
Count  of  all  orders  received  in  the  selected  period  across  all  sources  —  order  form,  manual  entry,  CSV  upload.  
Total  Orders  Delivered
 
Count  of  orders  with  delivery  status  marked  as  Delivered.  Revenue  is  only  counted  on  delivered  orders.  
Delivery  Rate
 
(Total  Delivered  ÷  Total  Generated)  ×  100.  Displayed  as  a  percentage.  Colour-coded  —  green  above  target,  amber  approaching  threshold,  red  below  minimum.  
Average  Order  Value  (AOV)
 
Total  sales  value  of  delivered  orders  ÷  Total  delivered  orders.  Only  uses  delivered  orders  —  undelivered  orders  have  not  generated  real  revenue.  
Platform  CPA
 
Ad  spend  ÷  Total  orders  generated.  This  is  what  Facebook  or  any  ad  platform  shows  as  the  cost  per  order.  Always  displayed  alongside  Real  CPA  so  the  gap  is  visible.  
Real  CPA
 
Ad  spend  ÷  Total  orders  delivered.  This  is  the  true  acquisition  cost  after  accounting  for  failed  deliveries.  Always  higher  than  Platform  CPA.  The  gap  between  the  two  is  shown  explicitly.

Average  COGS
 
Total  cost  price  of  all  delivered  orders  ÷  Total  delivered  orders.  Accounts  for  bundles  and  multi-unit  orders  —  not  just  the  single  unit  price.  Average  Delivery  Fee
 
Total  delivery  fees  paid  ÷  Total  delivered  orders. 
 
Profit  Per  Order
 
AOV  minus  Real  CPA  minus  Average  COGS  minus  Average  Delivery  Fee.  The  single  most  important  number  on  the  dashboard.  
Total  Profit
 
Profit  Per  Order  ×  Total  Delivered  Orders.  Cross-checks  against  revenue  minus  all  expense  categories. 
 
ROAS  vs  Business  ROI  —  Always  Separate  These  are  two  different  numbers  and  must  always  be  displayed  separately  with  clear  
labels.
 
They
 
must
 
never
 
be
 
merged
 
or
 
one
 
used
 
as
 
a
 
substitute
 
for
 
the
 
other.  
ROAS
 
Return  on  Ad  Spend.  Total  revenue  from  delivered  orders  ÷  Total  ad  spend.  This  is  the  advertising  metric.  A  strong  ROAS  does  not  mean  the  business  is  profitable.  
Business  ROI
 
Total  profit  after  all  expenses  (operational,  building,  marketing,  advertising)  ÷  Total  investment.  This  is  the  real  business  metric.  Always  calculated  and  shown  after  all  expense  categories  are  applied. 
 
Delivery  Rate  by  Location  The  dashboard  already  has  a  section  for  Delivery  Rate  by  Location  —  Lagos  vs  Outside  
Lagos.
 
This
 
must
 
show
 
delivery
 
performance
 
separately

Lagos
 
orders
 
and

because
